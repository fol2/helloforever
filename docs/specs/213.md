# Spec: #213 Strip format 2

Format 2 of the live-state strip. Format 1 is `docs/STRIP.md`; `docs/specs/29.md` gives its
reasons. Format 2 carries the same state in a shorter row, and has room for a unit table,
auras and cooldowns. The owner agreed the design on 18 Nov 2026 (#213): a keyframe each
second with deltas, ids with a hash, a unit table with party and raid, no social data. This
spec fixes the bytes. Each choice was measured on a real record.

Status: draft for build (#213, step 6). A line marked **Measured** comes from the records,
probes and screenshots of 20 Nov 2026 (#213 and its comments of that day). A line marked
**To measure** is an assumption; section 11 lists what settles it.

## 1. What exists and what changes

Format 1 sends the whole fast block (58 bytes) in every frame. It also sends one chunk of
one object in every frame, the objects strictly in turn. Format 2 changes four things:

1. **Fast data as keyframe and delta.** A keyframe holds every known group. It comes at
   least once a second. A frame between two keyframes holds only the groups that differ
   from the last keyframe. This replaces decision 2 of `docs/specs/29.md` ("every frame is
   whole").
2. **Objects on change.** A new object version goes first. A slow refresh sends the held
   objects again for a reader that starts late. A frame is only as long as it needs. This
   replaces decision 3 of `docs/specs/29.md` (the strict carousel).
3. **Squares of 1 pixel.** The row is 1 pixel high, each square 1 pixel wide.
4. **More units.** Target of target and the pet's target go in the fast data. Party, raid,
   bosses and nameplates go in an object.

These do not change: the finder, the four level triples of format 1, the session squares,
the palette, 6 bits a square with the fall-back to 3 bits, the check, the slot files, and
the object formats 0, 1, 254 and 255. The objects of format 1 keep their ids and their
JSON. HelloLink draws one format at a time. A reader reads both.

## 3. Decisions

1. **1-pixel squares, a row 1 pixel high.** **Measured**: 1 pixel reads as well as 2.
   The size stays a setting (`/hlink strip size 1 | 2 | 3 | 4`). The default becomes 1. A
   reader takes the square's side from the finder's runs, as now.
2. **6 bits a square stays the most.** **Measured**: 5 levels a channel read with none
   wrong, but their nearest colours came back 5 apart. One colour moved 6 in an hour. 6 and
   8 levels give colours that come back the same. The fall-back to 3 bits is unchanged.
3. **The palette stays.** The agreed design put the finder and a short ramp in place of
   the 64-colour palette, "a deeper colour only when a capture proves the colours exact".
   **Measured**: this display moves one channel by another (sent 0 came back as 0 to 46).
   A ramp of single channels cannot predict that. So the palette stays until a capture
   shows that a ramp predicts all 64 colours within 6 (section 11).
4. **A keyframe at least once a second; deltas against it.** A delta depends on its
   keyframe only, never on the frame before it. A lost delta costs nothing. A lost keyframe
   costs at most a second. The owner, 18 Nov 2026: "we can do 1 second keyframe. that's
   more than enough".
5. **Position in a delta is the difference from the keyframe**, as two zigzag varints
   (section 6). A map change, and a known position when the kept keyframe has none, send
   a keyframe at once.
6. **Target of target and the pet's target in the fast data** (groups 15 and 16). #213
   asks for self, target and target of target every 0.1 s. An object cannot give that: a
   new version waits 1.9 s at the 99th percentile (decision 7).
7. **Objects on change, then a slow refresh.** One chunk of at most 128 bytes a frame.
   Every 8th decision is a refresh chunk; the others send new versions first (section 7).
   **Measured** (simulated frame by frame on the record): new versions wait 0.1 s at the
   median, 1.9 s at the 99th percentile, 5.4 s at most. The refresh goes round all held
   objects in about 58 s.
8. **Objects keep JSON and deflate.** The agreed design put a binary schema in place of
   JSON and split text from numbers. **Measured**: with objects sent on change, the object
   part is 21 bytes of a 44-byte frame (section 10). A binary schema would save part of
   those 21 bytes. It would also change every object and every reader. It waits until a
   record shows the objects as the larger part.
9. **A new object format, 253: "secret now".** The addon read the object before, and the
   client keeps it secret now (auras in combat). A reader keeps its last copy, marks it
   old, and does not drop it. Format 255 keeps its meaning: cannot read, drop your copy.
10. **A unit table** (object 24): party, raid, bosses, focus, mouseover and nameplates,
    with what the probe read in combat. No health or power numbers: the client keeps them
    secret. The level squares carry health, as now (section 8). No position for other
    units: the client gives none.
11. **Ids with a 16-bit hash; the reader learns names** (object 25). Every reference is
    its kind, its id and the hash of its name. A reader keeps a cache keyed by (kind, id)
    and learns names from object 25. A hash that differs from the cached one tells the
    reader that the name changed. The owner, 18 Nov 2026: "id add hash can be ok".
12. **Auras (object 26) and cooldowns (object 27).** Auras are format 253 in combat.
    Cooldown times are `null` in combat; their `active` and `onGCD` flags stay.
13. **No social data**: no chat, whispers, friends or guild. Another player in the unit
    table has no name and no GUID, only a hash of the GUID to tell players apart. The
    owner, 18 Nov 2026: "we don't need social it's nothing to do with our features but for
    B, not only team/group but also raid".
14. **No error-correcting code yet.** **Measured**: 0 failed frames in about 166,000
    captures at 6 bits a square. The check stays the 16-bit sum of format 1. A reader keeps
    the chunks it has of an object across refresh rounds, and fills the gaps.

## 4. The row

| From | Squares | Part |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 8 | finder, as format 1 |
| 8 | 12 | four level triples, as format 1 (`docs/STRIP.md`, section 4) |
| 20 | 1 | depth and format: the digit 6 (format 2, 6 bits a square) or 3 (format 2, 3 bits a square) |
| 21 | 6 | session, as format 1 |
| 27 | 64 | palette, only with the digit 6 |
| 27, or 91 with a palette | 2 | `more`: the number of level triples that follow, as two-level digits (6 bits, most significant first) |
| then | 3 × `more` | the further level triples (section 8) |
| then | to the frame's end | the frame (section 5); black after it |

A reader reads the depth square first. Format 1 has the digits 4 and 2 there, format 2
the digits 6 and 3. A row of squares of exactly 1 pixel is read only when that square says
format 2: format 1 still refuses squares under 2 pixels (`docs/STRIP.md`, section 1). So a reader knows the layout before it reads a frame byte, and a
format-1 reader refuses a format-2 row (it accepts only 4 and 2). The frame's version byte
must agree with the depth square: 2 with 6 or 3. Refuse a frame where it does not.

## 5. The frame

Numbers are unsigned and big-endian, unless a field says otherwise.

| Bytes | Field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | version | 2. Refuse another. |
| 2 | session | As format 1. It must equal the session squares. |
| 1 | dwell | As format 1: how long a frame stays at least, in hundredths of a second, 5 to 100. |
| 2 | seq | One more, wrapping, each time the frame's bytes change. |
| 1 | kind | 128: a keyframe. 0: a delta. Refuse another value. |
| 1 | keySeq | The low byte of the `seq` of the keyframe that this frame's fast part refers to. A keyframe gives its own. |
| 1 | fastLen | Bytes of the fast part. |
| fastLen | fast part | Section 6. |
| 1 | objectId | 0: no object part. Then no more bytes come before the check. |
| 2 | objectVersion | Only when `objectId` is not 0. |
| 1 | chunkIndex | `0 ≤ chunkIndex < chunkCount`. |
| 1 | chunkCount | 1 to 12. |
| 1 | chunkLen | 1 to 255. The addon sends at most 128. |
| chunkLen | chunk | As format 1 (`docs/STRIP.md`, section 6). |
| 2 | check | As format 1, over every byte before it. |

Report nothing from a frame whose version, kind, lengths, session, dwell or check is wrong.

## 6. The fast part

The groups 1 to 14 and their fields are those of format 1 (`docs/STRIP.md`, section 5), in
the same order and sizes. Two groups are new:

| # | Group | Fields | Bytes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | targettarget | as group 7 (`target`): level, flags, name hash | 4 |
| 16 | pettarget | as group 7 | 4 |

A **mask** is 3 bytes. Bit 1 (group 1) is the most significant bit of the first byte; bit
9 is the most significant bit of the second byte. Bits 17 to 24 are 0 now; later groups
take them.

**Keyframe**: the mask `known`, then the fields of each known group, in group order. An
unknown group takes no bytes. (In format 1 it takes its bytes as zeros.)

**Delta**:

1. The mask `changed`: a bit for each group that differs from the keyframe that `keySeq`
   names. A group that became unknown is changed. So is a group that became known.
2. `gone`: one bit for each changed group, in group order: 1 for a group that is unknown
   now. The first bit is the most significant bit of the first byte. 0 bits fill the last
   byte. With no changed group, `gone` has no bytes.
3. The fields of each changed group that is known now, in group order. Group 9, `position`,
   has this form in a delta:

| Field | Encoding |
|---|---|
| dx | `x` now minus `x` in the keyframe, as a zigzag varint |
| dy | the same for `y` |
| facing | 1 byte, as format 1 |

The map is the keyframe's. A **zigzag varint** of a whole number `d`: `z = 2d` for `d ≥ 0`,
`z = -2d - 1` for `d < 0`. Then `z` goes out in groups of 7 bits, the lowest group first.
Each group is one byte; its top bit is 1 when another byte follows.

**The addon's rules.** Send a keyframe:

- as the first frame after a load, and after a change of format or depth;
- when the client's clock (group 1) shows a second with no keyframe yet, and when a second
  has passed since the last keyframe by the addon's own clock (the client's clock can be
  unknown or stand still);
- when the map changes;
- when `position` is known and the kept keyframe has none.

A delta compares each group with the keyframe as sent, not with the frame before it.

**The reader's rules.**

- Keep the last keyframe, the low byte of its `seq`, and the time it came.
- A delta whose `keySeq` is not that byte: drop its fast part. Its object part is good.
- Drop the kept keyframe when the session changes, when the row's depth square changes, and
  when it is more than 2 s old. The low byte comes round again after 256 frames: 25.6 s at
  the default dwell, 12.8 s at the shortest. The 2 s limit keeps an old keyframe from a
  false match.
- The state is the kept keyframe with the delta's groups put over it. A delta does not
  change the kept keyframe.

**Example.** A keyframe with `seq` 0x1234 sent `position` as map 1435, x 30000, y 40000,
facing 64. In a later delta the player is at x 30005, y 39998, facing 66, and the target
became unknown. Nothing else changed. The fast part:

| Bytes | Field |
|---|---|
| `02 80 00` | `changed`: group 7 (0x02 of the first byte) and group 9 (0x80 of the second) |
| `80` | `gone`: group 7 is unknown (1), group 9 is known (0), then six 0 bits |
| `0A` | dx 5: z 10 |
| `03` | dy -2: z 3 |
| `42` | facing 66 |

So `fastLen` is 7 and `keySeq` is `34`.

## 7. Objects

**Sending.** The addon keeps the newest version of each object, and whether it has sent
that version whole (each chunk once). It builds the objects afresh in turn, one on every second
decision (each about every 4.6 s, as often as format 1's carousel reads them), and only an
object marked changed is built. Each frame carries at most one chunk:

1. On every 8th decision: the next chunk of the refresh. The refresh goes round all held
   objects in id order, each version whole, and then starts again. When the object it is
   on gets a new version, the round moves on to the next object: the new version goes as
   new. (Every 8th decision, whatever waits: changes that never stop cannot starve it.)
2. Else, a version not yet sent whole: the next chunk of the one that waits longest.
   Object 24 goes before the others when two wait.
3. Else: no object part (`objectId` 0).

A decision is made once a dwell when the row has no chunk or a refresh chunk, and when a new
version's last chunk has had its time; the chunks of one new version follow each other with
no decision between them. A change of the
chunk's length (a new size, place or depth) gives every object a new version, so that a
reader that holds a version cut the old way takes the new chunks.

A chunk holds at most 128 bytes. One version is never sent in pieces of two versions.

**Formats.** 0, 1, 254 and 255 as format 1. The object bytes are the format byte, the
content, then the check of `docs/STRIP.md`, section 3, over both (section 6 there).

- **253, secret now.** No content. So the object bytes are `FD FD FD`: the format byte and
  its check. The reader keeps its last copy of that object, marks it old and shows it as
  old.
- Going into 253 makes a new `objectVersion`. Coming out of it does too, even when the
  content is the same as before 253. The reader takes that version as the fresh copy.

**References.** A thing with an id is written `"<kind>:<id>:<hash>"`. The kinds: `C` a
creature (the npc id in its GUID), `P` a player or a player's pet, `S` a spell, `I` an
item, `Q` a quest. The
hash is the 16-bit check of `docs/STRIP.md`, section 3, over the name's UTF-8 bytes, as one
number 0 to 65535. A player or a player's pet has no id and no name here: its id is the
same check over its GUID's UTF-8 bytes, and its hash is 0.

**Object 24, `units`** (sent on change, before the others):

```json
{"party1": ["P:51234:0", 38, "PRIEST", "normal", 10, "C:740:10475", 0, null, null],
 "boss1": ["C:740:10475", -1, "WARRIOR", "worldboss", 6, "party1", 3, 8, "S:133:9392"],
 "nameplates": [["C:740:10475", 35, "MAGE", "normal", 6, "pet", 3, null, null]]}
```

Each unit that exists, by its unit token: `focus`, `mouseover`, `party1` to `party4`,
`partypet1` to `partypet4`, `raid1` to `raid40`, `boss1` to `boss5`; and `nameplates`, a
list in the client's order. The player, pet, target, target of target and the pet's target
are in the fast data, not here. A unit is `[who, level, class, classification, flags,
target, threat, mark, cast]`:

- `who`: a reference.
- `level`: the client's level, -1 for a level it hides.
- `class`, `classification`: the second value of `UnitClass`; `UnitClassification`.
- `flags`: dead 1, in combat 2, can attack 4, connected 8.
- `target`: the unit's own target. The token of a unit in this table, or `player`, `pet`
  or `target`, when it is one of those. Else its reference. `null` for none.
- `threat`: `UnitThreatSituation("player", unit)`, 0 to 3, or `null` when the client gives
  none or keeps it secret.
- `mark`: the raid target mark, 1 to 8, or `null`.
- `cast`: the spell it casts or channels, as a reference, or `null`. **To measure**: whether
  the client gives another unit's cast.

**When the objects are built** (HelloLink 0.10.0): object 24 every 0.5 s (on every 5th
decision, besides its turn); 25 every 5 s; 26 and 27 when marked each second, on their turn
(each id about every 5.4 s with 27 ids). The cost, in `tests/strip_perf_test.lua` with an
action bar of 51 spells: 11.0 KB of memory a second, against format 1's 12.1. **To measure**
in the game: the cost with a full party, a raid and a crowd of nameplates.

**Object 25, `names`** (refresh only): `{"C:740": ["Adolescent Whelp", 10475], ...}`. The
names and hashes of the creatures, spells and items that the fast data and objects 24, 26
and 27 named in the last 5 minutes. A reader caches each by its key.

**Object 26, `auras`**: `{"player": [["S:14320:26398", 0, 0, 0, "player"], ...], "target":
[...]}`. Each aura is `[spell, stacks, duration, expires, source]`, from the client's
`C_UnitAuras.GetAuraDataByIndex` (`spellId`, `applications`, `duration`, `expirationTime`,
`sourceUnit`), helpful ones first. Format 253 while the client refuses the read.

**Object 27, `cooldowns`**: `{"slots": [[1, "S:14323:49069", true, false, 0, 0], ...]}`. Each
used action slot is `[slot, what, active, onGCD, start, duration]`: `what` is the spell, a
macro's spell, or the item (`I:`), `null` for anything else. `start` and `duration` are
`null` while secret (in combat). Only the first 48 used slots.

## 8. The level squares

The four triples of format 1 stay where they are: player health, player power, target
health, pet health. After the palette come `more` and then `more` further triples, in this
order: target of target health, then party1 to party4 health. The client colours each
triple, as in `docs/STRIP.md`, section 4. A reader that wants fewer reads the first ones and
skips the rest by `more`.

HelloLink 0.9.0 always draws five further triples (a unit that is not there is black), so
the row does not move when a party forms. Raid health waits for the curve probe in a raid
(section 11). The four triples of format 1 and the target of target's are coloured 10
times a second (the frames' rate), the party's 5 times: format 1 colours its four 20 times a
second, so format 2 asks the client's curves no more often, also in a party.

## 9. Readers

- Read the depth square first (section 4). It names the format and the depth.
- Keep keyframes as section 6 says. Show the age of the fast state: the time since the
  kept keyframe came. Past 1.5 s, say that the state is old.
- Keep the chunks of an object across refresh rounds. The next round fills a gap.
- Keep the name cache on disk, keyed by (kind, id). Replace an entry whose hash changes.
- `hellolink replay` reads a format-2 record as it reads format 1.

## 12. Acceptance (step 6)

- HelloLink draws format 2 when its setting says so (`/hlink strip format 2`). Format 1
  stays the default until a record of play with format 2 has 0 failed frames.
- Hand-written vectors in `tests/vectors/`: a keyframe; the delta of section 6's example;

  test makes them byte for byte.
- `hellolink` reads format 2 (`watch`, `replay`, `/state`). It keeps keyframes as section 6
  says. It gives the same state as format 1 for the same game state.
- **Reaction**: a change of a fast group shows in the next frame, as in format 1 (0.1 s at

- **CPU and memory**: no more than format 1. The memory test of #176

  format 1 for the same play script.
- A record of play with format 2 shows the bytes of section 10 within 20 %.

## 13. How the numbers of section 10 were made

The record stays on the owner's Mac: it holds the character's state. The steps, so that a
later record can be measured the same way:

1. `hellolink replay <record>` gives each new `seq` with its fast block, as JSON lines.
2. Fast data: the first frame of each second of the record's time is a keyframe (a 3-byte
   mask and its known groups at format-1 sizes). Each other frame is a delta against it:
   the 3-byte mask, the `gone` bytes, and each changed known group at its format-1 size.
   For `position`: the two zigzag varints and the facing byte. A map change, and a position
   that became known, make a keyframe, as section 6 says.
3. Objects: each frame's object part, read from the record's frame bytes, gives the object,
   version, chunk index and length. The bytes of a version are the sum of its distinct
   chunks. The sending of section 7 is then simulated frame by frame, at the record's own
   frame times. A version joins the queue at its first frame. Each frame sends at most one
   chunk. The refresh sends the held versions in id order.
4. The object part counts 6 bytes and the chunk for a frame with a chunk, and 1 byte
   (`objectId` 0) for a frame with none.
5. The frames are those of the second play of 20 Nov 2026, 18:45 to 19:46 (`t` 10,781 to
   14,453 in the record).
