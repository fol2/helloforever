# Spec: #213 Strip format 2

Format 2 of the live-state strip (`docs/STRIP.md` is format 1; `docs/specs/29.md` gives its
reasons). Format 2 carries the same state in a shorter row, and has room for a unit table,
auras and cooldowns. The design was agreed with the owner on 18 Nov 2026 (#213: a keyframe
each second with deltas, ids with a hash, a unit table with party and raid, no social data).
This spec fixes the bytes, with each choice measured on a real record.

Status: draft for build (#213, step 6). A line marked **Measured** comes from the records,
probes and screenshots of 20 Nov 2026 (#213, its comments of that day). A line marked **To
measure** is an assumption; section 11 lists what settles it.

## 1. What exists and what changes

Format 1 sends the whole fast block (58 bytes) in every frame, and one chunk of one object
in every frame, the objects strictly in turn. Format 2 changes three things:

1. **Fast data as keyframe and delta.** A keyframe holds every known group; it comes at
   least once a second. A frame between keyframes holds only the groups that differ from
   the last keyframe. This replaces decision 2 of `docs/specs/29.md` ("every frame is
   whole").
2. **Objects on change.** A new object version goes first; the rest of the room refreshes
   the objects a reader already holds. A frame is only as long as it needs. This replaces
   decision 3 of `docs/specs/29.md` (the strict carousel).
3. **Squares of 1 pixel.** The row is 1 pixel high, each square 1 pixel wide.

What does not change: the finder, the level squares, the depth square, the session
squares, the palette, depth 4 (6 bits a square) with the fall-back to depth 2, the check,
the slot files, and the object formats 0, 1, 254 and 255. The objects of format 1 keep
their ids and their JSON. HelloLink draws one format at a time; a reader reads both, by
the version byte.

## 3. Decisions

1. **1-pixel squares, a row 1 pixel high.** **Measured**: 1 pixel reads as well as 2
   (4 levels: 0 wrong at both). The size stays a setting (`/hlink strip size 1 | 2 | 3 | 4`);
   the default becomes 1. A reader takes the square's side from the finder's runs, as now.
2. **Depth 4 stays the most.** **Measured**: 5 levels a channel read with none wrong, but
   their nearest colours were 5 apart, under the 6 that one colour moved in an hour. 6 and
   8 levels give colours that come back the same. The fall-back to depth 2 is unchanged.
3. **A keyframe at least once a second; deltas against it.** A delta depends on its
   keyframe only, never on the frame before it, so a lost delta costs nothing and a lost
   keyframe costs at most a second. The owner, 18 Nov 2026: "we can do 1 second keyframe.
   that's more than enough".
4. **Position in a delta is the difference from the keyframe**, as two small numbers
   (zigzag varints, section 6). A map change sends a keyframe at once.
5. **Objects on change, then refresh.** New versions are sent first, in order of id. Room
   that is left refreshes the held objects in turn. A frame carries at most 128 bytes of
   chunk. **Measured** (simulated on the record): new versions wait 0.75 s at the 99th
   percentile and 1.6 s at most, against up to one carousel round in format 1.
6. **A new object format, 253: "secret now".** The addon could read the object before, and
   the client keeps it secret now (in combat, for auras). A reader keeps its last copy,
   marks it old, and does not drop it. Format 255 keeps its meaning (cannot read: drop).
7. **A unit table** (object 24): the player, pet, target, target of target, focus,
   mouseover, party, raid, bosses and nameplates, with what the probe read in combat. No
   health or power numbers: the client keeps them secret. The level squares carry them, as
   now (section 8).
8. **Ids with a 16-bit hash, names learnt by the reader** (object 25). A unit, aura or
   cooldown is sent as its kind, its id and the hash of its name. A reader keeps a cache
   keyed by (kind, id), and learns names from object 25. A hash that does not match tells
   the reader that the name changed. The owner, 18 Nov 2026: "id add hash can be ok".
9. **Auras (object 26) and cooldowns (object 27).** Auras are format 253 in combat.
   Cooldown times are `null` in combat; their `active` and `onGCD` flags stay.
10. **No social data**: no chat, whispers, friends or guild. Other players in the unit table
    carry no name and no GUID, only a hash of the GUID to tell them apart. The owner, 18
    Nov 2026: "we don't need social it's nothing to do with our features but for B, not
    only team/group but also raid".
11. **No error-correcting code yet.** **Measured**: 0 failed frames of about 166,000
    captures at depth 4. The check stays the 16-bit sum of format 1. A reader keeps the
    chunks it has of an object across refresh rounds and fills the gaps (a reader change
    only).

## 4. The row

The same as format 1 (`docs/STRIP.md`, section 2), squares of `size` pixels (default 1),
the row `size` pixels high. The level squares grow (section 8). The frame follows the
palette at depth 4, or the session squares at depth 2.

## 5. The frame

Numbers are unsigned, big-endian, unless a field says otherwise.

| Bytes | Field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | version | 2. Refuse another. |
| 2 | session | As format 1. |
| 1 | dwell | As format 1: how long the frame stays at least, in hundredths of a second. |
| 2 | seq | One more, wrapping, each time the frame's bytes change. |
| 1 | kind | 128: a keyframe. 0: a delta. Refuse any other value. |
| 1 | keySeq | The low byte of the `seq` of the keyframe that this frame's fast part refers to. A keyframe gives its own. |
| 1 | fastLen | Bytes of the fast part. |
| fastLen | fast part | Section 6. |
| 1 | objectId | 0: no object part, and no more bytes before the check. |
| 2 | objectVersion | Only when objectId is not 0. |
| 1 | chunkIndex | As above. |
| 1 | chunkCount | 1 to 12. |
| 1 | chunkLen | 1 to 255; the addon sends at most 128. |
| chunkLen | chunk | As format 1 (`docs/STRIP.md`, section 6). |
| 2 | check | As format 1, over every byte before it. |

Report nothing from a frame whose version, kind, lengths or check is wrong, or whose session
differs from the row's session squares.

## 6. The fast part

The groups and their fields are those of format 1 (`docs/STRIP.md`, section 5), in the same
order and sizes, groups 1 to 14. Bit 1 of a 2-byte mask is the most significant.

**Keyframe**: `known` (2 bytes: one bit a group), then the fields of each known group, in
group order. An unknown group takes no bytes (in format 1 it takes its bytes as zeros).

**Delta**: `changed` (2 bytes: one bit for each group that differs from the keyframe that
`keySeq` names, known or not), then `gone` (one bit for each changed group, in group order,
1 for a group that is unknown now; padded with 0 bits to whole bytes), then the fields of
each changed group that is known, in group order. Group 9, `position`, takes this form in a
delta:

| Field | Encoding |
|---|---|
| dx | `x` now minus `x` in the keyframe, as a zigzag varint |
| dy | the same for `y` |
| facing | 1 byte, as format 1 |

The map is the keyframe's: the addon sends a keyframe when the map changes. A **zigzag
varint** of a whole number `d`: `z = 2d` for `d ≥ 0`, `z = -2d - 1` for `d < 0`; then `z` in
groups of 7 bits, the lowest group first, each group in one byte whose top bit is 1 when
another byte follows.

**The addon's rules**: a keyframe when no keyframe was sent this second of the client's
clock, when the map changes, and as the first frame after a load. A delta compares each
group with the keyframe as sent, not with the frame before it.

**The reader's rules**: keep the last keyframe and the low byte of its `seq`. A delta whose
`keySeq` is another drops its fast part (its object part is still good). The state is the
keyframe with the delta's groups put over it; a delta does not change the kept keyframe.

**Example.** A keyframe with `seq` 0x1234 sent `position` as map 1435, x 30000, y 40000,
facing 64. A later delta: the player moved to x 30005, y 39998, facing 66, and nothing else
changed. Its fast part: `changed` = `00 80` (group 9), `gone` = `00` (one bit, 0, padded),
then position: dx 5 → z 10 → `0A`; dy -2 → z 3 → `03`; facing `42`. So `fastLen` = 6 and
`keySeq` = `34`.

## 7. Objects

**Sending.** The addon keeps, for each object, the newest version and whether a reader has
had it whole since it changed (sent once, all chunks). Each frame carries one chunk: the
next chunk of the lowest id with a version not yet sent whole; when none waits, the next
chunk of the refresh, which goes round all held objects in id order. A chunk holds at most
128 bytes. An object of one version is still never sent in pieces of two versions.

**Formats.** 0, 1, 254 and 255 as format 1. **253**: secret now. No bytes after the format
byte. The reader keeps its last copy of that object, marks it old, and keeps showing it as
old. 253 is not a new version of the content: when the client lets the addon read again,
the next version replaces the old copy.

**Kinds and name hashes.** A thing with an id is written `"<kind>:<id>"`. Kinds: `C` a
creature (the npc id in the GUID), `P` a player (no id: the 16-bit check of section 3 of
`docs/STRIP.md` over the GUID's UTF-8 bytes), `S` a spell, `I` an item, `Q` a quest. A
name's hash is that check over the name's UTF-8 bytes, as one number 0 to 65535.

**Object 24, `units`** (sent on change):

```json
{"player": ["P:4711", 39, "HUNTER", "normal", 6, null, 0, null],
 "target": ["C:740", 35, "MAGE", "normal", 4, "pet", 3, null],
 "nameplates": [["C:740", 35, "MAGE", "normal", 4, "pet", 3, null]]}
```

Each unit that exists, by its unit token (`player`, `pet`, `target`, `targettarget`,
`focus`, `mouseover`, `party1` to `party4`, `partypet1` to `partypet4`, `raid1` to
`raid40`, `boss1` to `boss5`), and `nameplates` as a list in the client's order. A unit is
`[who, level, class, classification, flags, target, threat, mark]`:

- `who`: kind and id. A creature's name is learnt from object 25.
- `level`: the client's level, -1 for a level it hides.
- `class`, `classification`: the client's file names (the second value of `UnitClass`;
  `UnitClassification`).
- `flags`: dead 1, in combat 2, can attack 4, connected 8.
- `target`: the unit's own target: the token of a unit in this table when it is one, else
  its `who`, else `null` for none.
- `threat`: `UnitThreatSituation("player", unit)`, 0 to 3, or `null` when the client gives
  none or keeps it secret.
- `mark`: the raid target mark, 1 to 8, or `null`.

**Object 25, `names`** (refresh only): `{"C:740": ["Adolescent Whelp", 12345], ...}`: the
names and their hashes of the creatures, spells and items that objects 24, 26 and 27 named
in the last 5 minutes. A reader caches each by its key. Players have no entry (decision 10).

**Object 26, `auras`**: `{"player": [["S:14320", 0, 0, 0, "player"], ...], "target":
[...]}`: each aura as `[spell, stacks, duration, expires, source]`, the client's
`C_UnitAuras.GetAuraDataByIndex` (`spellId`, `applications`, `duration`, `expirationTime`,
`sourceUnit`); helpful then harmful. Format 253 while the client refuses the read.

**Object 27, `cooldowns`**: `{"slots": [[1, "S:14323", true, false, 0, 0], ...]}`: each
used action slot as `[slot, spell, active, onGCD, start, duration]`; `start` and `duration`
`null` while secret (in combat). Only the first 48 used slots.

## 8. The level squares

The level squares of format 1 carry four values in three squares each (`docs/STRIP.md`,
section 4). Format 2 puts a count before them: two squares, as two-level digits (6 bits,
most significant first), the number of triples that follow. Their order: player health,
player power, target health, pet health, target of target health, party1 to party4
health, then raid1 to raid40 health while in a raid group (in place of the party). A
reader that knows fewer reads the first ones and skips the rest by the count.

## 9. Readers

- Read the version byte first: 1 is `docs/STRIP.md`, 2 is this spec.
- Show the age of the fast state: the time since the kept keyframe. At more than 1.5 s,
  say that the state is old (a keyframe was lost).
- Keep the chunks of an object across refresh rounds; a gap is filled by the next round.
- Keep the name cache on disk, keyed by (kind, id); drop an entry whose hash changes.
- `hellolink replay` reads format-2 records as it reads format 1.

## 10. What the choices cost

From the record of 61 minutes of play (section 2). Bytes are mean bytes a frame.

| Part | Format 1 | Format 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Frame head, object part head and check | 15 (7 + 6 + 2) | 17 (9 + 6 + 2); 12 with no object part |
| Fast data | 58 | 11.4 (keyframes 52 on average, 45 to 58; deltas mostly 2 to 10) |
| Fast data, position not as a difference | | 13.1 |
| Object chunks | 167 (every frame, the carousel) | 16.4 (new versions, then a refresh of all objects every 60 s) |
| The whole frame | 240 | about 45 |
| Fixed squares (finder, levels, depth, session, palette) | 91 | 96 alone (the count and target of target added); 12 more in a party, 120 more in a raid |
| Row length at 6 bits a square | 411 squares, 822 pixels at 2 pixels | 156 squares, 156 pixels at 1 pixel |

The longest format-2 frame: a keyframe of 58 bytes and a chunk of 128, 203 bytes, a row of
367 pixels alone. A chunk budget of 64 bytes would wait 1.6 s at the 99th percentile and 5.1 s at
most; 32 bytes, 6 s and 14 s. 128 bytes keeps the wait under 2 s.

The new objects (24, 26, 27) are not in the record. **To measure** in step 6: their bytes
and how often they change in combat. An estimate: a solo unit table of 5 units is about
300 characters of JSON before deflate; it changes several times a second in a fight.

## 12. Acceptance (step 6)

- HelloLink draws format 2 when its setting says so (`/hlink strip format 2`); format 1
  stays the default until a record of play with format 2 has 0 failed frames.
- Hand-written vectors in `tests/vectors/`: a keyframe, a delta with a position difference
  (the example of section 6), a frame with no object part, and an object of format 253.
  The reader reads them; the addon test makes them byte for byte.
- `hellolink` reads format 2 (`watch`, `replay`, `/state`), keeps keyframes as section 6
  says, and gives the same state as format 1 for the same game state.
- A record of play with format 2 shows the bytes of section 10 within 20 %.

## 13. How the numbers of section 10 were made

The record stays on the owner's Mac (it holds the character's state). The steps, so that a
later record can be measured the same way:

1. `hellolink replay <record>` gives each new `seq` with its fast block, as JSON lines.
2. Fast data: the first frame of each second of the record's time is a keyframe (2 bytes
   and its known groups at format-1 sizes). Each other frame is a delta against it: 2 bytes
   of `changed`, the `gone` bits, and each changed known group at its format-1 size, or for
   `position` the two zigzag varints and the facing byte (7 bytes on a map change).
3. Objects: each frame's object part, read from the record's frame bytes, gives the object,
   version, chunk and length. The bytes of a version are the sum of its distinct chunks.
   Send on change is simulated a second at a time: the new versions of that second join a
   queue, 10 frames send up to the chunk budget each, and the refresh adds 6,108 bytes
   (all held objects) every 60 s in the room left. The wait is the queue's bytes over the
   bytes a second.
4. The frames are those of the second play of 20 Nov 2026, 18:45 to 19:46 (`t` 10,781 to
   14,453 in the record).
