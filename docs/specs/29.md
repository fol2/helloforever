# Spec: #29 Live-state strip

One row of coloured squares and bars at the top edge of the game. It carries the
character's live state out of the running game, with a public format. It is an
**interface with more than one reader**: the assistant of this repository
(`docs/north-star.md`, priority 3) and other programs. The owner, 12 Nov 2026: "you are
not the only user. i'll expand to other use cases". This repository owns the addon side
and the format, and adds no control of the game (`docs/owner-decisions.md`, 2026-11-12).
What a reader does with the data is the reader's business.

Status: draft for build. A line marked **Measured** was seen in the game on 12 Nov 2026 and
is recorded in `plan/05-assistant-link.md`, sections 3 and 4. A line marked **To measure**
is an assumption; section 11 lists what settles it.

## 1. What exists and what changes

HelloLink 0.0.2 draws one typed text for 30 s (format 0). This spec replaces it with
format 1: an always-on row that holds the whole fast state in every frame and carries
larger or slower data in turns. The typed text becomes one object of format 1. Format 0 and
the probe commands are removed when format 1 ships.

The strip stays in the separate addon `HelloLink`. HelloForever's capture never depends on
it, and a player can switch the strip off without losing fight data.

## 2. Decisions

1. **One row, at the very top-left, 3 pixels high** (2 and 4 can be set). Owner: "1 line
   only and can do 3/2 px start from 0,0 so it won't interrupt normal plays".
   **Measured**: squares of 8, 4, 3 and 2 physical pixels are drawn as exact squares and
   read without an error.
2. **Every frame is whole.** A reader takes screenshots at its own pace and misses frames.
   No frame depends on an earlier one. Change-only updates (deltas) are rejected for that
   reason.
3. **Slow data rides a carousel.** Each frame also carries one chunk of one larger object.
   Every current object is sent again at least every 30 s, so a reader that starts late or
   misses a chunk still gets everything.
4. **Two colour levels a channel, three bits a square.** **Measured**: pure colours come
   back within 2 of 255, but blue came back as (0, 46, 255): a display's colour management
   moves the values in between, so more levels are not safe.
5. **Values the client keeps secret go out as bars.** **Measured**: the player's health and
   power, the target's health and maximum health and the pet's health are secret to an
   addon at all times, also out of combat; an addon cannot test, compare or compute on
   them. The client lets an addon hand such a value to a status bar (its own unit frames do
   that), and a reader measured four such bars in a fight in agreement with the game's own
   numbers. Threat goes out the same way (owner, 12 Nov 2026: "there's threat meter"),
   whether it turns out secret or not (**To measure**). Owner: "that's a part of our box features, i prefer real live". The addon passes
   the value from the API straight to the bar and never does anything else with it.
6. **Unknown is a value.** A field the addon cannot read is marked unknown in the frame.
   The addon never guesses.
7. **Text is compressed** with the client's `C_EncodingUtil.CompressString` (Deflate) when
   the client has it (**To measure**); else it goes uncompressed and the object says so.

## 3. The row

All positions are in physical pixels from the row's top-left corner. `S` is the side of a
square: 3 by default. The addon sizes everything as `pixels * 768 / physicalHeight /
UIParent:GetEffectiveScale()` UI units (**Measured** to give exact pixels).

| Part | From x | Width | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| finder | 0 | 8 S | The digits `4 2 1 7 0 6 3 5` (red, green, blue, white, black, yellow, cyan, magenta). A digit is `4*red + 2*green + blue`, each channel fully on or off. |
| gap | 8 S | 4 | Black. |
| bar 1 to 6 | 8 S + 4 + 104 (k - 1) | 100, then 4 black | White from the left over the fraction `value / maximum`, black after it. Bar 1: player health. Bar 2: player power. Bar 3: target health. Bar 4: pet health. Bar 5: the player's threat on the target, 0 to 100 per cent of what pulls it. Bar 6: the pet's threat on the target, the same. A bar whose unit does not exist is all black. |
| second finder | 8 S + 628 | 8 S | The finder's digits in reverse: `5 3 6 0 7 1 2 4`. |
| frame | 16 S + 628 | to the end of the frame | The frame of section 4, three bits a square, most significant bit first. Black after the frame's end. |

**The second finder gives the scale.** A screenshot can show the row at another scale than
the game drew it (a scaled window, a display with a backing scale). The eight squares of
the finder cannot give that scale. **Tested on drawn images, not in the game**
(`tests/hellolink_strip_test.py`, with the four bars of the probe): at a scale of 0.99
every square of the finder still measures 3 pixels, and bars placed from that read 49, 98,
22 for 50, 100, 25. The second finder starts exactly `8 S + 628` pixels after the first, so
the distance between the two in the screenshot, divided by that number, is the scale. With
a second finder the same test read the bars to within 2 per cent at each scale from 0.60 to
4.00 in steps of 0.05. `S` is the one of 2, 3 and 4 whose `8 S + 628` agrees with the
finder's own squares. In the game only a scale of 1 was seen.

The row is `S` pixels high. The frame must fit the width of the game: at 3 pixels a square,
a game 1920 pixels wide holds 414 squares after the second finder (155 bytes), one 2560 wide
holds 628 (235 bytes). The addon makes the object chunk as long as fits (section 6); a frame with
no chunk is 70 bytes (187 squares), which fits any game 1237 pixels wide or more.

The default position is (0, 0) of the game's UI. `/hlink strip on|off`, `/hlink strip at
<x> <y>` and `/hlink strip size <2|3|4>` change it; the addon keeps the settings. The row
is in the `TOOLTIP` strata at a high frame level: it covers what is under it. Hiding the
whole UI hides the row.

A reader must not assume the position, `S` or the scale: it finds the two finders and may
then capture only the row's rectangle (section 8).

## 4. Frame

All numbers are unsigned and big-endian. `uN` is N bits.

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| version | u8 | 1. A reader refuses a version it does not know. |
| session | u16 | 1 to 65535, chosen at random each time the UI loads. A reader that sees it change forgets every object and partial object it holds. |
| seq | u16 | Goes up by one, wrapping, each time the frame's bytes change. |
| fastLen | u8 | Bytes of the fast block. A reader skips bytes it does not know at its end. |
| fast block | fastLen bytes | Section 5. |
| object | 6 bytes + chunk | Section 6. |
| check | 2 bytes | Section 7, over every byte of the frame before it. |

**When the frame changes.** The addon reads the fast state 10 times a second and redraws
the squares that changed. The carousel moves on its own clock (section 6). The fast block's
clock field changes every second, so `seq` goes up at least once a second while the game
runs: a reader that sees no new `seq` for 3 s treats what it holds as stale. The bars are
not part of the frame: the client redraws them itself, and they change without a new `seq`.

**Later versions.** Version 1 only grows: new bytes at the end of the fast block, new
`known` bits, new object ids, new keys in an object's JSON. A reader ignores what it does
not know. A change of the row's layout, the finder, the bit order, a frame field above or
the meaning of an existing field is a new version, announced in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## 5. Fast block (version 1, 56 bytes) and the bars

`known` has one bit for each group below, in the order given, the first group in the most
significant bit; the unused low bits are 0. A group whose bit is 0 has all its bytes 0 and
is unknown: unread, secret, or not present (no target, no pet).

| # | Group | Fields | Bytes |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | `known` u16 | 2 |
| 1 | clock | `time()` modulo 65536, u16 | 2 |
| 2 | health | maximum health u24 | 3 |
| 3 | power | maximum power u24, power type u8 (the client's power type number) | 4 |
| 4 | level | level u8, XP u24, XP for the level u24 | 7 |
| 5 | state | u16 of flags: in combat 128, dead or ghost 64, mounted 32, resting 16, AFK 8, casting or channelling 4, has target 2, has pet 1, loot window open 256; the other bits are 0 | 2 |
| 6 | cast | spell id u24, tenths of a second left u8 | 4 |
| 7 | target | level u8, flags u8 (hostile 128, dead 64, elite 32, rare 16, tapped by another 8, a player 4, its own target is the player 2, its own target is the player's pet 1), name hash 2 bytes | 4 |
| 8 | pet | maximum health u24 | 3 |
| 9 | position | map id u16, x u16, y u16, facing u8 | 7 |
| 10 | purse | money in copper u32, free bag slots u8 | 5 |
| 11 | hand | main-hand item id u24 (0 = empty), seconds left of its temporary enchant u16 (0 = none) | 5 |
| 12 | loot | items-received counter u8 (goes up by one, wrapping, for each item the player receives), the last item's id u24, its quantity u8 | 5 |
| 13 | uiError | the client's number of the last UI error message u16, error counter u8 (goes up by one, wrapping, for each error message) | 3 |

Conversions, all done by the addon:

- A number larger than its field holds is sent as the field's largest value.
- `x`, `y`: the map fraction (0 to 1) as `floor(fraction * 65535 + 0.5)`.
- `facing`: `floor(radians / (2π) * 256 + 0.5) modulo 256`, from the client's
  `GetPlayerFacing()`. **To measure**: which way is 0 and which way it grows.
- `tenths of a second left`: `ceil(seconds left * 10)`, 0 to 254; 255 means more than
  25.4 s. While channelling, the cast group holds the channel's spell and time.
- `seconds left` of the temporary enchant: `ceil(seconds)`, at least 1 and at most 65535
  while there is one. 0 only when there is none.
- target `level`: 0 is unknown, 255 is a level the client hides (skull).
- The two counters let a reader that misses frames still count events: the difference
  between two readings, modulo 256, is how many happened in between. A reader that was away
  for 256 events or more cannot tell.
- `name hash`: the two check bytes of section 7 over the UTF-8 bytes of the target's name.
  The name itself is object 2.

**The bars** are part of the fast state. They have no `known` bit: bars 3, 5 and 6 mean
something only when `has target` is set, and bars 4 and 6 only when `has pet` is set; with
the `dead` flag set a bar reads 0. The current value is about the bar's share of the
group's maximum (the target's maximum health is secret, so only its share is known).

How a reader measures bar k. With the scale `c` of section 3 and the row's left edge at
`x0` in the screenshot, the bar runs from `a = round(x0 + (8 S + 4 + 104 (k - 1)) c)` to
`b = round(x0 + (8 S + 104 k) c)`, and `L = b - a` is its length in pixels. In the pixels
from `a - 1` to `b` inclusive the bar must be: at most two black pixels, then `w` white
pixels, then black to the end; anything else is unreadable in that screenshot (something
covers the bar). The reading is `min(100, round(100 w / L))` per cent. One pixel is
`100 / L` per cent: 1 at a scale of 1, 1.7 at 0.60. The bars carry no check: a cover that
happens to leave "white, then black" gives a wrong reading that nothing detects.

## 6. Objects (the carousel)

The object part of every frame:

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| objectId | u8 | Table below. 0 = none: the other five bytes are 0 and no chunk follows. |
| objectVersion | u16 | Starts at a random number when the UI loads; goes up by one, wrapping, when the object's content changes, at most once a second for one object. |
| chunkIndex | u8 | From 0. |
| chunkCount | u8 | Chunks in this version of the object, 1 to 24. |
| chunkLen | u8 | Bytes that follow: 1 to 160, and no more than fits the row. |

The chunks of one version, joined in index order, are the **object bytes**:

| Part | Size | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| format | 1 byte | 0 = the content is UTF-8 text. 1 = the content is the Deflate of UTF-8 text. 254 = the object is too large to send (more than 24 chunks of the length that fits this game's width): no content follows. 255 = the addon cannot read this object now (an API is missing or returned a secret value): no content follows. For 254 and 255 a reader drops the copy it holds. |
| content | the rest but 2 | Empty for formats 254 and 255. |
| object check | 2 bytes | Section 7, over the format byte and the content. |

**Which chunks belong together.** An object version is named by `(session, objectId,
objectVersion)`. That name is not used twice while a reader can still hold a piece of it:
the addon changes one object's version at most once a second, so a version number comes
round again after 65536 seconds at the earliest, and a new session picks new random
numbers. A reader keeps one partial object for each `objectId` and throws it away when
`session` changes, when a chunk of that id arrives with another `objectVersion` or
`chunkCount`, or when 30 s pass without a new chunk of it. The object check then only
guards against a misread chunk; it is not what keeps versions apart.

**How the addon sends.** One chunk is on the row for 0.3 s, then the next. The addon sends
whole objects, one after the other, from a queue of the current objects:

- It takes the objects in turn. When an object's content changes, the object moves to the
  front of the queue, behind the one being sent; one object may move forward that way at
  most once in 10 s, so an object that changes all the time cannot hold the others back.
- A question goes to the front at once, behind the one being sent.
- An object is at most 24 chunks (7.2 s). A larger one is sent as format 254.

What follows from that, with at most six objects: a changed object's first chunk is on
the row within 7.2 s, and at once (within 0.1 s) when nothing is being sent or the row
shows an object's last chunk; every current object is sent again at least every 90 s (a
whole round is at most 43.2 s, and moves to the front can make an object wait one more
round). A reader treats an object it has not seen whole for 180 s as stale. For an event
that must be seen at once, the fast block has counters (the `loot` and `uiError` groups);
the object is the full picture a few seconds later.

An object is current from the first time it has content until the UI reloads; "nothing
there" is content (`null`, `[]`), not absence.

The objects of version 1. The text of every object but 1 is JSON, UTF-8, with exactly the
keys shown; a reader ignores keys it does not know.

| Id | Object | Text | Changes when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | question | Plain text, not JSON: what the player typed with `/hlink send`. | The player sends. It is current for 30 s, then it leaves the carousel. Version 1 has no acknowledgement: a reader acts once for each `(session, objectVersion)` and remembers the ones it acted on for as long as the session lasts (the same pair can only come back after 65536 questions). How an answer comes back is the assistant's spec. |
| 2 | target | `{"name": "Wetlands Crocolisk", "type": "Beast", "class": "normal", "level": 23}` or `null` with no target. `type` is the client's creature type or `null`. `class` is one of `normal`, `elite`, `rare`, `rareelite`, `worldboss`, `trivial`, `minus`. `level` is -1 when hidden. | The target changes. |
| 3 | place | `{"map": 1437, "zone": "Wetlands", "sub": "Black Channel Marsh"}`; `sub` is `""` when the client names none. | Zone or subzone changes. |
| 4 | bags | `{"free": 12, "items": [[2318, 14], [4234, 3]]}`: each pair is an item id and how many the bags hold of it, ordered by item id. | A bag changes. Several changes inside one second make one version. When it reaches the row is the queue's rule above; the `loot` group of the fast block reports a received item at once. |
| 5 | quests | `[{"id": 290, "title": "Lifting the Curse", "state": "in_progress", "objectives": [{"text": "Crude Flint", "have": 0, "need": 9, "done": false}]}]`, ordered by id. `state` is one of `accepted`, `in_progress`, `ready`, `failed`, `unknown`, with the meanings of `docs/SCHEMA.md`. `have` and `need` are `null` for an objective without a count. | The quest log changes, at most once every 2 s. |

| 6 | skills | `[{"id": 182, "name": "Herbalism", "rank": 75, "max": 150}]`: every skill line the client lists with a rank, ordered by id. | A rank or a maximum changes. |

## 7. The check

Two sums over bytes, both starting at 0: for each byte `b`, `s1 = (s1 + b) modulo 255`,
then `s2 = (s2 + s1) modulo 255`. The two check bytes are `s1`, then `s2`, in that order
(as HelloLink's format 0 writes them). Test vector: the five bytes of `abcde` give
`F0 C8`. The frame check, the object check and the target's name hash all use it.

## 8. Readers

Anyone may write a reader. What a correct one does:

1. Find the finder in a screenshot and the second finder on the same row; take the scale
   and `S` from the distance between them (section 3); refuse a row without its second
   finder, and measure the bars as section 5 says. From then on capture only the row's rectangle. Locate again when two captures in
   a row do not decode.
2. Accept a frame only when the finder, the version, the lengths and the frame check are
   all right. Report nothing from a frame that fails.
3. Read the bars from the same screenshot as the frame, and use bars 3 to 6 only with the
   frame's `has target` and `has pet` flags.
4. Treat a group whose `known` bit is 0, and an object of format 254 or 255, as unknown,
   not as zero or empty.
5. Treat the state as stale after 3 s without a new `seq`.
6. Keep no screenshot.

This repository's reader is `tools/hellolink_strip.py`, standard-library Python:
`watch [--json]` prints one line for each new `seq` (the fast block, the bars, and each
object when it completes); `read --screen | --image` reads one frame. **Measured** with no strip on
screen: about 7 captures a second of a 600 by 80 rectangle, each searched for a row (the
search, not a decode of a found frame); a capture of three displays takes about 0.7 s.

When format 1 ships, the format moves from this spec into `docs/STRIP.md`, the standing
reference for readers (as `docs/SCHEMA.md` is for the saved data), with test vectors: the
bytes of one frame and the image of its row, which this repository's tests also read.
`AGENTS.md` routes to it. A change to the format changes that file in the same pull
request.

## 9. Limits and non-goals

- No control of the game: nothing here presses a key, casts or moves. The row only shows
  what the addon can read or what the client already draws; a reader only looks.
- No memory reading, no packet reading. The row is on screen and the player can switch it
  off.
- The row is in any screenshot or stream the player shares; `/hlink strip off` before
  sharing.
- Cost: the addon rebuilds the frame at most 10 times a second and recolours only squares
  that changed. Target: under 0.2 ms a rebuild, measured in the build.
- Not in version 1: auras, the rest of the gear (HelloForever's snapshot has it at a reload), cooldowns,
  action bars, group members, an acknowledgement of a question.

## 10. Acceptance (from the issue)

- This document: square size, frame layout, field table with types and units, updates,
  compression, sequence and loss handling, versioning, position. Sections 2 to 7.
- A test that round-trips every field of the fast block, the six bars and a chunked
  object (compressed and not, and format 255) through an image, as
  `tests/hellolink_strip_test.lua` does for text; the reader's refusals (unknown version,
  bad lengths, bad frame check, bad object check, mixed versions, a session change, an
  object of 25 chunks) in
  `tests/hellolink_strip_test.py`; the test vector of section 7.
- In the game: `watch` follows a minute of play; every frame it reports passed the check;
  known fields and the bars agree with what the game shows; the owner confirms that the row
  at (0, 0) covers nothing he uses.
