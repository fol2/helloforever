# Spec: #232 Strip format 3 (a proposal)

Status: **proposal for the owner**. Nothing here is built. Each part names the measure that
must come first (LESSONS: probe a client capability before a feature stands on it). A line
marked **Measured** comes from format 2's records and probes (#213, `docs/specs/213.md`); a
line marked **Estimate** is a number to check.

The owner, 21 Nov 2026: "propose format 3, the much more advanced stream than format 2 and
1, higher bandwidth, lower compute/memory, full rust, more comprehensive data, rust
reader/decoder (if possible) (can still have swift recording)".

## 1. Where format 2 stops

| Limit | Format 2 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Squares | One row, 1 pixel high: room for about 2,400 squares on this display (an estimate from its width) | `docs/specs/213.md` |
| Colour | 6 bits a square (4 levels a channel). 5 levels come back 5 apart; 6 and 8 levels collide | **Measured**, `plan/05` P6 |
| Capture | sRGB, 8 bits a channel (`tools/strip_capture.swift`: `colorSpaceName = sRGB`, 32BGRA) | the code |
| Rate | At most 10 frames a second at the default dwell (0.1 s); 6.2 new frames a second in play | **Measured**, #231 |
| Captures | About 39 a second: 83,910 captures in 2,163.8 s (38.8 a second), from the first format-2 frame of the record of 20 Nov 2026 to its last | **Measured**, the record |
| Loss | In play: 257 of about 13,600 seq values (1.9 %) never in that record (two large seq jumps left out). A lost keyframe makes the state old until the next one: keyframe gaps, the stops of the reader left out, 1.0 s at the median, 1.91 s at the 99th percentile, 2.58 s at most. Standing for an hour (21 Nov 2026): 44 of 14,764 (0.3 %); keyframe gaps 1.09 s at the 99th percentile, 2.03 s at most | **Measured**, #213 (its comment of 21 Nov 2026) |
| Object part | 128 bytes a frame; a new version waits 1.9 s at the 99th percentile | **Measured** (simulated on the record) |
| Addon work | Objects are JSON built from Lua strings, then deflated by the client: most of the strip's memory | `tests/strip_perf_test.lua` |
| Units with health | Five through the client's colour curves (level squares) | `docs/specs/213.md`, section 8 |

Format 2 sends about 51 bytes a frame. It is far from its ceiling in steady play; the limits
bite in bursts (a quest list, a raid, a city of nameplates) and in what the addon spends to
make the bytes.

## 2. Rules for format 3

1. **Probe first.** A part is built only after its probe shows the client and the capture
   allow it.
2. **The reader's interface does not change.** The same command line (`watch --json --serve
   --addons --remember --record --names`, `assist`), and the same `/state` and `/events`
   shapes, with fields added only. The local program needs no change to keep working.
3. **Rust outside the game, Lua inside it.** The game runs only Lua, so the addon stays Lua.
   Everything else is Rust: the schema, the decoder, the reader, the tools, and the code that
   makes the addon's encoder tables. The capture may stay the small Swift tool.
4. **No more work in the addon than format 2.** `tests/strip_perf_test.lua` plays the same
   script in format 2 and format 3 and holds: memory a second, the client calls, and the Lua
   time to pack a frame and to paint it (a raid of 40 included). Format 2's figure there is
   11.2 KB a second (HelloLink 1.0.0, its ring of draw times included). The Lua time is judged a second, not a frame: at twice the frames a second, the same time a frame is twice the cost. The test prints the time a second (`tests/strip_perf_test.lua`).
5. **Formats 1 and 2 stay readable.** The depth square names the format (format 3: a new
   digit pair, section 5).
6. **No social data**, as format 2 (decision 13 of `docs/specs/213.md`).

## 3. The proposal in one table

| Part | What | Gain | Measure first |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | One schema, in Rust, for every record; binary records in place of JSON objects | Less addon memory and work; smaller objects | Re-encode the objects of the 36-minute record with the schema (offline) |
| B | Lanes: 1 to 4 rows of 1-pixel squares | Up to 4× the squares a frame | Read a 4-row probe from the stream |
| C | Deeper colour from a better capture | 6 to 9 bits a square (1.5×) | `/hlink probe depth 8 1` with the capture in the display's colour space |
| D | 20 frames a second, with forward error correction | 2× the rate; a lost frame repaired | Capture misses at a dwell of 0.05 s; the ring of draw times |
| E | More state: every unit's record each frame, action bars as bit sets, an event ring, windows | The assistant's data | The probes of #213 (most are readable), a raid |
| F | A Rust decoder crate the local program links | One decoder for every reader | The reader and the local program's Rust engine decode the same vectors to the same records |

Together, **Estimate**: A to D give about 4 × 1.5 × 2 = 12 times format 2's ceiling, for
bursts; the steady stream stays small. Each factor is only counted once its probe passes.

## 4. The parts

### A. One schema, in Rust; binary records

Today each object is JSON text, built in Lua (strings, `string.format`, `table.concat`) and
deflated by the client. That is the strip's largest cost in the addon.

Format 3 describes every record once, in a Rust schema (`reader/hellolink-format/schema`):
a record's fields, their types (unsigned of n bits, signed, a reference `kind:id:hash`, a
short text with a length, a list), and their order. From it, a Rust tool makes:

- the decoder (Rust);
- the addon's encoder tables (Lua: for each record, its field list and bit widths), so that the
  addon packs values into a kept byte buffer with no string and no table a frame;
- the docs (a field table a record) and the test vectors (and the hand-written ones stay).

A change to a record is a change to the schema; the Lua tables and the Rust decoder cannot
drift apart. Text stays only where it is text (a quest's objective, a guide step). A record
names a text by its hash; the texts go round in a slow refresh, as format 2's names do, so a
reader that started late or missed a frame gets them on the next round.

**Estimate**: objects shrink well below JSON plus deflate for numeric records (bags, gear,
units, cooldowns), and the addon asks for no memory to build them. **Measure first**: a Rust
tool re-encodes the objects 2 to 23 of the format-2 record of 20 Nov 2026 (23:13 to 23:49, 36
minutes, `hellolink replay`) with the schema and prints the bytes against format 2's. No
game needed. Objects 24 to 27 are not in that record: they are measured from the addon test's
play and from the first play of HelloLink 1.0.0.

### B. Lanes: more rows

Format 2 is one row of 1-pixel squares. Format 3 may draw `lanes` rows under it (1 to 4),
each 1 pixel high, the frame's bytes running along lane 1, then lane 2. The row stays the
same width; with 4 lanes it is 4 pixels high, still a thin line at the top edge (the owner,
12 Nov 2026: "1 line only and can do 3/2 px"). The lane count is in the depth square's
neighbour, so a reader knows the layout before the frame (section 5).

**Measure first**: a probe draws 4 lanes of a known pattern; the reader reads them from the
capture stream at scale 1 and counts wrong squares.

### C. Deeper colour

The capture asks for sRGB (`colorSpaceName = sRGB`); the measured collisions (blue leaks into
green) look like the display's conversion to sRGB. Three ways, to try in this order:

1. The capture in the display's own colour space (`CGColorSpace` of the display, or none),
   and a reader that learns each colour from the palette as now.
2. A palette that leaves out the colours that collide (format 2's 5-level probe read 0 wrong;
   only the nearest two were close).
3. Per-channel calibration ramps beside the palette, so that a reader models the display.

At 8 levels a channel a square is 9 bits (1.5× format 2's 6). The palette grows from 64 to
512 squares. It is drawn once a second, in a lane of its own. Its cost is one lane, once a second.

**Measure first**: `/hlink probe depth 6 1` and `8 1` read with the capture in the display's
colour space (a flag of `strip_capture`); 0 wrong and the nearest colours at least 12 apart
(format 2's fall-back threshold) over an hour.

### D. Twenty frames a second, repaired

Format 2's dwell is 0.1 s. Format 3 may halve it, if the capture keeps up: the stream gave
about 39 captures a second in play (**Measured**, the record). Two repairs, for two faults:

- **A frame captured with a few wrong squares**: a short Reed–Solomon parity in the frame (for
  example 8 bytes for 4 byte errors) repairs it in place. It cannot help a frame that no
  capture got.
- **A frame no capture got**: only a later frame can stand in. Keyframes drawn for two dwells,
  or an XOR parity frame every n frames that rebuilds one lost frame of those n. A reader
  reports a gap it cannot fill (a seq it never got, an event counter that jumped).

The 1.9 % of frames never captured is not yet explained. HelloLink 1.0.0 keeps when each
frame went up, by the addon's clock (`HelloLinkDB.frames`, the ring of draw times); set
beside a record, it tells a frame that was up too short from a capture that came late. That
decides between the two.

**Measure first**: the ring of draw times against a record (one play); a record at a dwell of
0.05 s, with the miss rate.

### E. More state

The format-2 probe (#213, `plan/05` section 26) showed what the client gives in combat. Format
3 sends, as binary records of part A:

- **Units, each frame**: the target, target of target, focus, pet's target, party, and in a
  raid 40 members: level, class, flags, raid mark, target, threat (a few bytes each). Health
  through the client's colour curves for every one of them (level squares in a lane of their
  own), once the raid probe shows the curves work for raid units.
- **Action bars as bit sets, each frame**: usable, in range, active, on the global cooldown,
  for 48 slots: 24 bytes. The client gives these in combat (**Measured**).
- **Casts**: the target's and the focus's cast, when the client gives another unit's cast
  (**Measure first**: the probe's `unit` lines while a creature casts).
- **An event ring**: the last 8 events with a counter each (loot, XP, money, a quest step, a
  level, a death). A reader that missed frames still gets every event while fewer than 9 came
  in its gap; when the counter jumped by more than 8, it says how many events it lost.
- **Windows**: merchant, loot, quest dialogue, flight map, as format 2's bank and trainer
  objects, in binary.
- **The character**: talents, spellbook, professions and recipes, reputations, sent on change.

Still not sent: chat, whispers, friends, guild (no social data), and anything the client keeps
secret (health and power numbers; auras in combat: format 2's 253).

**Measure first**, each in the game before it is built:

| Item | Scene to see it in |
|---|---|
| Units of a raid, their curves | A raid group (the owner's) |
| Another unit's cast | A creature that casts (the probe's `unit` lines) |
| Action bars in combat | A fight: usable, in range, active per slot (the probe saw these readable) |
| Windows | A merchant, a loot window, a quest dialogue, a flight master |
| The character | A talent and a profession window open, a reputation change |
| The event ring | A fight with loot and XP, a quest turned in |

### F. A Rust decoder for every reader

The decoder becomes a crate of its own (`reader/hellolink-format`): bytes in, records out, no
screen, no file, no clock. The reader (`hellolink`) uses it, and so can the local program's
Rust engine directly, with the same records the reader serves. The capture stays Swift
(`tools/strip_capture.swift`), started by the reader as now.

## 5. The frame of format 3 (sketch)

| Part | Change from format 2 |
|---|---|
| Depth square | A new digit for format 3 (5 with the palette); the next square gives `lanes` (1 to 4) and the bits a square (6 or 9) |
| Palette | 64 colours, or 512 in a lane of its own at 9 bits |
| Frame | Format 2's head, plus a parity length; the body is schema records (part A) in place of groups and JSON chunks |
| Parity | Reed–Solomon over the frame, after the check |

The fast part keeps format 2's keyframe and delta rule; its groups become schema records.

## 7. The plan

| Step | Work | Needs the game |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The schema crate and a re-encoder: format 2's recorded objects as binary records; the bytes against JSON plus deflate | No |
| 2 | The ring of draw times against a record: why 1.9 % of frames were never captured | One play (HelloLink 1.0.0) |
| 3 | Probes: colour in the display's colour space (C), 4 lanes (B), a dwell of 0.05 s (D), a raid's curves (E) | Short game sessions |
| 4 | This spec, rewritten from the measures: what stays, with numbers | No |
| 5 | Build: the decoder crate and the Lua tables from the schema; the addon's packer; the reader. The memory and time test of rule 4 for format 2 against 3 | No |
| 6 | Vectors by hand, the addon test, a record of play; format 3 by setting, then by default | One play |

Steps 1 and 2 can start at once; step 1 needs no game, step 2 needs one play of HelloLink 1.0.0.

## 8. Not in format 3

- Window capture or an invisible strip (the owner plays full screen; the strip must show).
- A request channel from the reader to the addon faster than the slot files (the addon
  cannot read the screen or a socket; the slots stay, a few minutes apart).
- Social data.

## 9. Decisions for the owner

1. Lanes: may the strip grow from 1 to up to 4 pixels high (part B)?
2. Which steps to build after the probes (part E's list is long; the assistant's needs come
   first).
