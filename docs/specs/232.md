## 1. The owner's aims, in order

The owner, 21 Nov 2026, after the proposal: "Sure let's do format 3. For the game loss frame.
Probably the game itself frozen. Due to computer not good. I always got “stuck” in game. So
format 3 need 2 things to support, 1 is least compute possible don't drag down the game
performance further, and if loss frame, still able to have good recovery. Plus more bandwidth
and more comprehensive info".

1. **Least work in the game.** The game already stops for moments on this computer. The strip
   must not add to that.
2. **Good recovery from lost frames.** A frame that no capture got must cost the reader as
   little as possible, and the reader must be whole again soon after.
3. **More bandwidth.**
4. **More data.**

When two aims pull apart, the lower number wins. The proposal's 20 frames a second and its
Reed–Solomon parity are dropped for that reason (section 8).

## 3. Where format 2 spends its work in the game

| Work | When | Its cost grows with |
|---|---|---|
| Read the state (54 client calls a `sample()` in the test's stub) | each 0.1 s | the calls |
| Build an object (Lua strings, JSON, the client's deflate) | on a change | the object's text |
| Pack the frame and its check | each frame | the bytes |
| Paint: one `SetColorTexture` a square whose colour changed | each new frame | the squares that changed |
| Paint the level squares: the client's colour curve, one call and one `SetColorTexture` a square, with no cache (the colour is secret) | each level update | the units |

The paint is the part that grows with bandwidth. At 6 bits a square, a byte that changes
costs about 1.33 calls. Format 2's fast block changes length (a keyframe or a delta), so the
chunk and the check after it move and are painted again even when their bytes are the same.

## 4. The design

### A. Recovery: every frame stands alone

- **No deltas.** The fast block holds the full state, absolute, in every frame. A lost frame
  costs only its dwell (0.1 s by default); the next frame captured is whole. (Format 2: up to
  2.58 s.)
- **A directory in every frame**: three bytes a slow record, its id and its version (the same
  16 bits as the chunk's version). After a gap, the first frame tells a reader which records it holds old.
  A version comes round again only after 65,536 changes of one record; a reader that has not
  seen a record's version for 10 minutes holds it old whatever the directory says.
- **An event ring in every frame**: the last 8 events of the last 10 s (loot, a UI error, XP,
  money, a level, a death) and a counter of every event. A gap of fewer than 9 events in 10 s
  loses none when the row has room for them (a short row keeps the newest that fit; a row too
  short for the counter sends no ring, and the next ring says what came in between); when the counter jumped by more, the reader gets the events the ring keeps and
  reports how many it lost. A quest step is not in it: object 5 holds the quest log, and the
  directory says when it changed (section 6, section 3).
- **Slow records go changed-first**, then in a refresh round, as format 2's carousel. The
  round sends every record at least once in `R` seconds (`R` set by the measure of step 5,
  at most 30 s) (while the back-off holds and new versions keep the carousel busy, a round can take longer: the ACCEPTED limit of section 4 B, #258); a reader that missed a record waits at most one round.
- **A new version goes twice.** Each new version goes up a second time, about 1 s after the
  first. The addon cannot see a stop of the capture alone (the game runs on); one second's
  stop of the capture costs no record then. A longer stop costs at most one refresh round.
- **A stall sends again.** When the addon's update comes more than 0.25 s after the one
  before, the game stopped. The addon queues again the records whose chunks were up in the
  second before the stop. A stop of the game is exactly when a capture is most likely lost.
- **A version is cut into chunks once.** The chunk boundaries of a version are fixed when it is
  cut; a sending again uses the same cut. A new cut is a new version (as format 2), so a reader
  never joins chunks of two cuts.

**Target** (**Estimate**, measured at step 5, at the default dwell): the fast state whole and
the events the ring keeps in the first frame captured after a gap; a record changed during a
gap of 1 s or less whole within 1 s of the gap's end when the chunks changed in the gap fit
the chunk slots of that second (10 at the default dwell, 5 while the back-off halves the
rate); more chunks than that, or a longer gap, within one refresh round. Step 5 measures it
with records that compete for the slots, with and without the back-off.

### B. Least work

- **One clock.** A frame is drawn at most once a dwell, on one tick (`now >= nextFrame`, then
  `nextFrame = now + dwell`). Format 2 draws on two clocks (the carousel and the fast read);
  one clock makes a frame shorter than the dwell impossible by construction.
- **10 frames a second stays.** Twice the rate is twice every cost a frame, and a stopped game
  stops both rates alike.
- **A fixed layout.** The fixed part comes first, at fixed squares: the head, the fast block,
  the event ring, the directory. The chunk comes last. A value that does not change paints
  nothing; a chunk's length moves nothing before it.
- **Binary records, no text.** The addon packs schema records (part E) into byte tables it
  keeps: no JSON, no deflate, no new string or table a frame. Text stays only where it is text
  (a quest's objective), sent by hash and a slow round.
- **A paint budget.** At most a set number of squares are painted again a frame; a chunk is
  only as long as the budget allows. The number starts at format 2's largest frame (212 bytes,
  about 290 squares) and is set by the perf test (rule 4). The budget is fixed when a version
  is cut (part A), so the chunks of a version keep their size.
- **No slow update.** The total a second is not enough: one long update is a hitch the player
  sees. The perf test also holds the slowest update. Large work is spread: a record is packed
  in parts over several updates, and the level squares of a raid are painted in turn, at most
  10 units an update (40 units: each once in 4 updates, 0.4 s at the default dwell).
- **Back off when the game is slow.** While the game's frame rate is under 20 a second, a new
  chunk goes up every second dwell; the fixed part still goes every dwell. **Measured** (the API
  probe, `/hlink probe api`, seven runs on the level 1 test character in Stormwind, 20 and 21 Nov 2026, out of
  combat): `GetFramerate` gives a number, not secret: 16 to 27 frames a second on this Mac. Built
  in HelloLink 1.6.0: the addon reads it once a second (`S2.slow`). While it is under 20, the chunk
  moves on every second frame, counted in frames, not in time, so an update that comes late still
  leaves a chunk two frames, also when the rate goes up before they passed. **ACCEPTED limit**
  (review of #257): while the game is slow and new versions keep the carousel busy, the chunk
  slots halve and the refresh keeps its every 8th decision, so a refresh round takes longer (a
  reviewer's simulations at 17 frames a second: object 25 every 29.2 s became 32.8 s in one, and
  its whole sending every 45 s became 57 s in another). The
  directory still says at once which object is old (section 4 A), so a reader holds no old copy as
  fresh. A refresh at every 4th decision while slow was tried and taken out: with an idle carousel it
  doubled the refresh's chunks, more work while the game is slow. Step 5 sets `R` from the game
  (#258). Not seen yet: the value in a fight (`plan/05`, section 28).
- **Deeper colour is also a saving.** At 9 bits a square a byte costs about 0.89 calls, not
  1.33 (part C below).
- **The reader is on the same computer.** Its work also takes from the game. The capture keeps
  to the strip's rectangle and a set rate; the decoder asks for no memory a frame. The
  reader's processor use (2.2 % on 21 Nov 2026, `ps`) is measured at each step and must not
  grow.

The depth square says the format and the bits a square (section 6: the digit 5 for 6 bits with the drawn palette, 7
for 3 bits, 1 for the computed palette, whose levels square says 4 or 8 levels: 6 or 9 bits), and the square after it says
the lane count, so a reader knows the layout before the frame. The palette's order, the bits
inside the squares and the 0 bits that fill the last square are those of `docs/STRIP.md`,
section 2 (depth 4 for 6 bits, depth 2 for 3 bits). A row with a ramp
("The computed palette", below) needs no palette lane: the ramp is 10 or 22 squares of lane 1. The ceiling is for
bursts (a quest list, a raid, a merchant); the paint budget keeps steady play small. What
counts is new data delivered a second, measured at step 5, not the room on the screen.

**The fall-back stays** (`docs/owner-decisions.md`, 2026-11-12: "if unstable and received
certain error, then fallback to lower bit"). When frames fail their check or the palette's
nearest colours come too close, the reader asks through the slot file for fewer bits a square:
9 to 6, 6 to 3. Format 2's request always asks for 3 bits (`strip = { session, depth = 2 }`,
`reader/hellolink/src/slots.rs`); format 3's request names the bits wanted
(`strip = { session, bits = 6 }`), and the addon still reads `depth = 2` as 3 bits. The addon
takes a new depth by starting a new session (a new session number), as a change of size or
place starts the row afresh, so the palette and the layout never change inside a session. The
reader takes the new palette and layout from the first frame of the new session. At 3 bits the
palette is format 1's 8 colours and the depth square says 3 bits (section 6). The vectors of step 4 include a
frame at each depth.

#### The computed palette (owner decision, 22 Nov 2026)

The palette is computed, not drawn (`docs/owner-decisions.md`, 2026-11-22). The row carries a
short ramp. A reader builds every colour from the ramp.

**The formula.** With `L` levels a channel (4 for 6 bits, 8 for 9 bits), code `i` (0 to L³ − 1)
has red level `floor(i / L²)`, green level `floor(i / L) modulo L`, blue level `i modulo L`, as
in `docs/STRIP.md`, section 2. The addon draws level `k` at `lo + k × (1 − lo) / (L − 1)` of
full. `lo` is the floor: the lowest level is not black. The drawn values are the addon's to
choose. A reader never holds them; it takes each level from the ramp.

**The ramp.** `3 × (L − 1) + 1` squares: the codes 0; then L², 2L², … (red rising, green and
blue at level 0); then L, 2L, … (green); then 1, 2, …, L − 1 (blue). 10 squares at 4 levels
(codes 0, 16, 32, 48, 4, 8, 12, 1, 2, 3), 22 at 8 levels. The ramp is static: the addon paints
it once a session, and it is on the row in every frame, so every capture stands alone (part A).
It is in lane 1 only; the other lanes use it. The finder and the digit squares do not serve as
ramp points: they are fully on or off, and the palette does not use black. They stay two-level,
so the depth, the layout and the session are readable when the ramp is not.

**The reader.**

1. Read the ramp. `v[c][0]` is channel `c` of the first square. `v[c][k]` is channel `c` of the
   square that drives channel `c` at level `k`.
2. If a channel's values do not rise strictly, the row has no frame (`why`: `ramp`).
3. Name a frame square channel by channel: the level whose `v[c][k]` is nearest to the square's
   channel `c`; of two equally near, the lower. The code is `(red × L + green) × L + blue`. This
   is the nearest colour of the computed palette `(v[red][i], v[green][j], v[blue][k])` by the
   sum of squared differences, at the cost of three look-ups. A reader keeps three tables of 256
   entries and builds them again when the ramp as read changes.
4. `gap` is the smallest `v[c][k] − v[c][k − 1]` over the three channels. `leak` is the largest
   distance, over the ramp squares, of a channel that the square does not drive from that
   channel's `v[c][0]`. Both are in capture units (0 to 255). They replace `apart` for a row with
   a ramp.
5. A frame counts as failed when its check fails, when its ramp does not rise, or when
   `gap − 2 × leak` is under 12. A frame whose check holds is still used. For a row with a ramp,
   the reader asks for fewer bits when more than 2 in 100 of its last 200 frames failed (lead
   decision, #284). A row with a drawn palette keeps its rule: ask at once when `apart` is under
   12. The margin: half a gap, less the leak, leaves 6 units for noise, which is the 6 that one
   colour moved in an hour of play (#213) and the 12 of the drawn palette's rule.

The reader assumes no colour space and no curve: the ramp samples each channel at exactly the
levels the frame uses. It does assume that a channel's value does not depend on the other two.
`leak` shows where that fails on the ramp; the frame's check shows the rest.

**Why a floor.** **Measured** (22 Nov 2026, the three window screenshots of
`/hlink probe depth 8 1 4` and the three of `/hlink probe lanes`, sRGB, one display, read again
offline; the same in all three of each):

- Each channel alone: sent 0, 36, 73, 109, 146, 182, 219, 255 came back 0, 24 or 25, 60, 98,
  138, 179, 220 or 221, 255, within 1 of each other for the three channels. Whatever the other two held, red moved at most 7
  from its ramp and blue at most 4.
- One move is larger. With strong blue and weak red, green near black is lifted, up to 46. The
  lifted green is the larger of the two values, not their sum. Why is not known.
- All 15 wrong squares of 1,024 are green level 1 named green level 0 with blue at level 5 or
  more. 18 pairs of palette colours are under 12 apart, all with green level 0 or 1; 7 of them
  came back identical. No reader can tell an identical pair apart, drawn palette or computed.

| Levels used of the image | Pattern squares | Wrong, drawn palette | Wrong, ramp | `gap` | `leak` | `gap − 2 × leak` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 of 8 (floor 0) | 1,024 | 15 | 36 | 24 | 46 | −68 |
| 1 to 7 of 8 (floor 1/7) | 701 | 0 | 4 | 34 | 22 | −10 |
| 2 to 7 of 8 (floor 2/7, 216 colours) | 452 | 0 | 0 | 34 | 2 | 30 |
| 2, 4, 6, 7 of 8 (floor 2/7, 64 colours) | 142 | 0 | 0 | 34 | 1 | 32 |
| 4 of 4 (the lanes images, floor 0) | 1,024 | 0 | 29 | 73 | 46 | −19 |

So a ramp with black as level 0 is worse than the drawn palette, and a floor of 2/7 removes the
lift in these images. `lo` is 2/7 until the measures below settle it. **Estimate**, from the
curve above: 8 levels from 2/7 to full come back about 24 to 30 apart, so `gap − 2 × leak` is
about 20; 4 levels come back about 62 to 68 apart.

**Level spacing.** No reader tells the addon which intensities to draw. The measured crush is the
lift, not uneven steps: every step of a channel alone is 24 or more. Because a reader takes the
levels from the ramp, the addon can move them later (a floor for each channel, even steps in
capture units) with no change to the format or to a reader. That is built only when a measure
shows a `gap` under 12 with a small `leak`.

**The fall-back** is as above: 9 to 6 to 3 bits, by `strip = { session, bits }`. A row at 9 or 6
bits has a ramp; a row at 3 bits has none (digit 7, as now).

**Measure first** (rule 1). Until step 3 passes, the addon sends the drawn palette (digit 5).

1. No game: `hellolink depth` also reads a probe image by the ramp, which is among the palette
   squares the probe already draws (the codes above). It prints `rampWrong`, `gap` and `leak` for
   the whole image, for each floor `f` the same over the levels `f` to `L − 1`, and the same for a
   list of levels given on the command line (for example `--levels 2,4,6,7`). On the screenshots of
   22 Nov it must give the table above.
2. `/hlink probe depth <levels> [pixels] [lines] [floor per cent]` draws the levels from the
   floor up. Screenshots of `4 1 4 29` and `8 1 4 29`: pass is `rampWrong` 0 and
   `gap − 2 × leak` at least 12. This settles `lo`. If no floor passes at 8 levels, 9 bits is
   left out and this spec says so.
3. The capture stream: a row with a ramp at 6 bits, behind `/hlink strip ramp on`, with
   `--record`, for an hour. A capture with no row (`why`: `no row`) is counted apart and is not a
   failed frame. Pass: of the captures with a row, no more failed frames in 100 than the drawn
   palette's sessions, and `gap − 2 × leak` at least 12 in every record line that has a ramp. Then the ramp is the default at 6 bits.
4. 9 bits: the same hour at 8 levels, with the 9-bit rows behind a switch (`/hlink strip bits 9`),
   after the slot request with `bits` is built and lanes have passed on the stream. This is the hour of the lever table, not a second one.

**The row** (section 6) gains the digit 1, a levels square and the ramp. A reader of before this
change finds no row at the digit 1, so the reader is installed first. A row at 6 bits is 53
squares shorter than with the drawn palette.

**Vectors and tests.** `tests/vectors/strip3.json` holds frame bytes and does not change. A new
hand-written vector holds rows as captured colours, with squares before, after and under the row
as a screen has: a 4-level ramp at its drawn values, a 4-level ramp moved as measured above (61, 138, 221, 255,
the levels 2, 4, 6 and 7 of the 8-level image), squares 5 off their level, a square exactly between two levels, a ramp that does not
rise, and a leak that brings `gap − 2 × leak` under 12. Each case gives the codes, `gap`, `leak`
and the frame's bytes, worked out by hand. The addon's test holds the painted colours against
fractions written by hand (4 levels at `lo` 2/7: 6/21, 11/21, 16/21, 1). A record
(`--record`) has `ramp` in place of `palette`; `rows` and `watch` gain `gap` and `leak`, and
`apart` stays for a drawn palette (rule 2: fields added only). `docs/STRIP.md` gains, for a
second reader: the formula, the ramp's order, the naming rule with its tie rule, "do not assume
black is level 0, and do not assume the drawn values", the failed-frame rule, the `ramp` record
key and the new `hellolink depth` fields.

**Rejected, with the reason.** A middle rung of 6 levels (216 colours, 7.75 bits): the bits are
not whole, so the frame's bytes would need a second packing. A model of the lift in the reader:
a lift that depends on two channels needs L² calibration squares for each pair, and where two
colours come back identical no reader can recover them. Feedback of the drawn levels through the
slots: not needed, since a reader takes the levels from the ramp.

### E. One schema in Rust; one decoder

Every record is described once, in a Rust crate (`reader/hellolink-format`): its fields, their
types (unsigned of n bits, signed, a reference `kind:id:hash`, a short text with a length, a
list) and their order. From it a Rust tool makes the addon's encoder tables (Lua: for each
record, its field list and bit widths), the field tables of the docs, and test vectors. A
change to a record is a change to the schema; the Lua tables and the decoder cannot drift
apart. Hand-written vectors stay (LESSONS: check an encoder against bytes written by hand).

The same crate is the decoder: bytes in, records out, no screen, no file, no clock. The reader
(`hellolink`) uses it, and so can the local program's Rust engine. The capture stays the small
Swift tool, started by the reader as now. The addon stays Lua: the game runs only Lua.

minutes) piped into `cargo run -p hellolink-format --bin measure`. 476 versions of 20 objects of

the same from its binary form. Bytes, summed over the versions:

the learnt shapes keep as JSON (`Any`), texts included; their figure here sends those texts
in full, where a hand-written schema can type each place in the list. The largest by
reference, in bytes a version:
object 19 (533), 11 (465, guide steps whose texts carry the guide addon's UI codes, `|T...|t`
and `|c...|r`), 5 (452, quests) and 22 (446, prices).

**Binary objects** (object format 2; since HelloLink 1.2.0, format 3 only). An object with a

(the same values; compact, its keys in order), with `"format": 2` on the object. The shapes'
binary forms are in `lib.rs`'s head: whole numbers as zigzag LEB128, a reference as its id
(LEB128) and its 16-bit hash, a number with one decimal as ten times it, a text as its length
and UTF-8, a list as its count and items, a record as presence bits and its present fields.

## 5. Rules

1. **Probe first.** A part is built only after its probe shows the client and the capture
   allow it.
2. **The reader's interface does not change.** The same command line and the same `/state`
   and `/events` shapes, with fields added only.
3. **Rust outside the game, Lua inside it.**
4. **No more work in the addon than format 2.** `tests/strip_perf_test.lua` plays the same
   script in format 2 and format 3. It holds: memory and squares painted (level squares included) a
   second, and the client's colour-curve calls of the run. It prints, and does not hold, the Lua
   time a second and the slowest single update (the lead decision below). The script plays a party
   of four (format 2 there: 11.2 KB, 0.72 ms and 759 squares a second), then a raid of 40 with 12
   nameplates.

   **Measured** (the raid of 40 and 12 nameplates, #232, HelloLink 1.5.0, Lua 5.1 on the MacBook
   Pro, the same in three runs, 21 Nov 2026). The raid's levels change every 5 s, so object 24
   has new versions in the measured 30 s; each format draws versions of it whole, deflated:
   format 2 in 6 chunks, format 3 in 3 (the stub's deflate makes a text a sixth as long). A
   second, format 3 10.0 KB and 809 squares, format 2 14.1 KB and 948; colour-curve calls in the
   measured 30 s, 3,600 in both. The test holds these, and that each format's measured 30 s read
   the 40th member and the 12th nameplate. Printed: the Lua time a second, format 3 1.62 ms
   against 1.31 ms (24 % more), and the slowest update, 0.71 ms against 0.62 ms. The lead decision
   below covers it: the larger cost is likely the client's own work a painted square, and format 3
   paints fewer. Step 5 measures both in the game.

   **Measured** (step 4, the addon in the test's stub, on the MacBook Pro, 21 Nov 2026): format 3
   9.1 KB and 725 squares painted a second, 6,300 curve calls in the run of 35 s, the slowest update
   0.39 ms; format 2 11.2 KB, 759, 6,300 and 0.38 ms. The test holds the memory, the squares and the calls.
   The Lua time is 1.04 ms a second against format 2's 0.91 (14 % more): every frame is built
   whole (67 bytes of fast state and the directory, and their check), where format 2 builds a
   small delta. **Lead decision**: format 3 goes on with it. The difference is 0.13 ms of Lua in
   a second; the client's own work for each painted square, which the stub cannot time, is likely
   the larger cost (**Estimate**), and format 3 paints fewer squares. The test prints the Lua time and the slowest
   update; they are not held. The owner may refuse this, and step 5 measures both in the game.
5. **Formats 1 and 2 stay readable.** The depth square names the format.
6. **No social data.**

## 6. The frame of format 3

The core of step 4: 1 lane, 6 or 3 bits a square. Numbers are unsigned and big-endian, as in
format 2. The hand-written vectors are `tests/vectors/strip3.json`. What this section does not
change is as format 2 (`docs/specs/213.md`, sections 4 to 8): squares of 1 pixel are read, as
for format 2; the palette, the order of the bits in the squares and the black after the frame
are format 2's, which are `docs/STRIP.md`'s, section 2.

The chunks carry each object as object format 2 (binary, part E: "Binary objects") when the
object has a binary shape, and else as format 2's object bytes (object formats 0 and 1, JSON
plain or deflated). 253 to 255 are as format 2. Each object moves to binary in a pull request of
its own; rule 4 is measured at each move. Until all have moved, section 4 B's "no JSON, no
deflate" holds for the fixed part and the binary objects only.

**The row.**

| From | Squares | Part |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 8 | finder, as format 1 |
| 8 | 12 | four level triples, as format 2 |
| 20 | 1 | depth and format: the digit 5 (format 3, 6 bits a square, drawn palette), 7 (format 3, 3 bits a square) or 1 (format 3, computed palette: part C, "The computed palette"; not built) |
| 21 | 1 | layout: the digit `lanes - 1`. Only 0 (one lane) for now; a reader refuses another |
| 22 | 6 | session, as format 1 |
| 28 | 64 | palette, only with the digit 5 |
| 28 | 1 | only with the digit 1: the digit `L - 1`, 3 (4 levels, 6 bits) or 7 (8 levels, 9 bits). A reader refuses another |
| 29 | 3 × (L - 1) + 1 | only with the digit 1: the ramp, 10 or 22 squares |
| 28 (digit 7), 92 (digit 5), 39 or 51 (digit 1) | 2 + 3 × `more` | `more` and the further level triples, as format 2 |
| then | to the frame's end | the frame; black after it |

**The frame.**

| Bytes | Field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | version | 3. Refuse another. |
| 2 | session | As format 2. It must equal the session squares. |
| 1 | dwell | As format 2: 5 to 100 hundredths of a second. |
| 2 | seq | One more, wrapping, each time the frame's bytes change. |
| 2 | fixedLen | Bytes of the sections that follow. |
| fixedLen | sections | Each: an id (1 byte), a length (1 byte), that many bytes. Every frame has the fast section first and the directory second; other sections may follow them, each id at most once. A reader skips an id it does not know, and refuses a frame whose first two sections are not these. |
| 2 | check | As format 1, over every byte before it. A frame whose check fails gives nothing. |
| 1 | objectId | 0: no chunk, and the frame ends here. |
| 2 | objectVersion | Only when `objectId` is not 0. |
| 1 | chunkIndex | `0 ≤ chunkIndex < chunkCount`. |
| 1 | chunkCount | 1 to 12. |
| 1 | chunkLen | 1 to 255. The addon sends at most 128 (the paint budget, section 4 B). |
| chunkLen | chunk | As format 2. |
| 2 | chunkCheck | As format 1, over `objectId` to the chunk's end. A chunk whose check fails is dropped; the sections before it are good. |

The sections:

| Id | Section | Bytes | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | fast | 67 | The mask `known` (3 bytes, as format 2), then the fields of all 16 groups of format 2 in group order, each at its full size. An unknown group's bytes are 0. So every group is always at the same place (section 4 B). |
| 2 | directory | 3 a record | For each object that has a version, in id order: its id (1 byte) and its version (2 bytes). A reader holds an object old when its version differs. |
| 3 | event ring | 1 + the events | HelloLink 1.5.0. The counter (1 byte): one more, wrapping, for each event since the UI loaded. Then the kept events, oldest first, the newest being the counter's: the last 8 of the last 10 s. Each is a kind (1 byte) and its values: 1 loot (item 3, quantity 1); 2 a UI error (its number 2); 3 money gained and 4 money lost (copper 4); 5 XP gained (3); 6 a new level (1); 7 a death (none). A reader refuses a frame whose ring has a kind it does not know, an event cut short or more than 8 events. A row too short for the whole ring keeps its newest events and the counter; a row too short for the counter alone leaves the section out. A level goes in at the next frame, after the XP that made it. **ACCEPTED limit** (review of #255): an event of the client takes the time of the next frame drawn, so events while the strip is hidden (`/hlink strip off`) stay in the ring, the last 8 of them, and are said when it shows again; the reader says the rest as `ring-lost`. None is said twice. The addon takes them from what it reads anyway (the loot and UI error events of the client, and the level, purse and state groups from one frame to the next), so the ring asks the client for nothing. **The reader**: the first frame of a session only gives it the counter. After that, the counter's step from the frame before is the number of new events. When it passes the kept events, the reader says `ring-lost` with the count, then the events it has. A step of 128 or more is taken as a new start. |

Every frame is whole: there are no deltas, so a reader keeps no keyframe. The fast state is the
fast section of the last frame whose check held.

**The addon's rules.** One clock: a frame is built when `now ≥ nextFrame`, then `nextFrame =
now + dwell`. On each tick the carousel of format 2 makes its decision (section 7 of
`docs/specs/213.md`), and the chunk it gives stays up for that tick (two ticks for a chunk begun
while the game is slow, section 4 B). Two rules are added:

- When a new version has been sent whole, it is sent whole once again 1 s later, after any
  other new version. The refresh round keeps every 8th decision, as in format 2, so its bound
  (section 4 A) holds, except while the back-off halves the chunk slots of a busy carousel (#258).
- When an update comes more than 0.25 s after the one before, the newest version of every object
  that had a chunk up in the second before the stop is sent whole once again, as in the rule
  above. (When an older version was up, the newest is the one the reader needs.) A send once
  again that began before such a request does not end it: the request waits for a send that
  begins after it.

## 8. Not in format 3

- **20 frames a second.** It doubles every cost a frame (aim 1) and does not help when the
  game stops.
- **Reed–Solomon parity in the frame.** No frame of 28,063 failed its check: frames are lost
  whole, and a parity repairs only wrong squares. Its Lua work would come every frame.
- **Keyframes for two dwells, XOR parity frames.** Part A's absolute fast block, directory and
  sending again recover for less.
- Window capture or an invisible strip (the owner plays full screen; the strip must show).
- A request channel from the reader to the addon faster than the slot files.
- Social data.
